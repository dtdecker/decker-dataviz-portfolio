# Outline

For my final project, I am interested in arguing support for public transportation and modes of transport alternative to cars. To accomplish this, I am primarily interested in visualizing data which compares transit ridership to commuting times via car, and revealing that public transit is not only better for the environment but also more efficient in terms of travel. My reasoning for this focus is that while public transit has a base of support in the U.S, those who commonly argue against it often prefer car-dependent development. Therefore, I want to write a narrative and call-to-action that proves the benefits of public transportation to car users as well.

In this argument, I think the two primary audiences I will be writing to are community members and urban planners. Community members want to see their lives improve, which would include being able to travel to work and other destinations faster. If I show how reducing traffic and increasing transportation options accomplishes this, they would likely support the argument. Similarly, urban planners also need to be convinced that all forms of transportation are beneficial, and that increasing access to public transportation will reduce dependencies on car-centric development. This argument should gain support as well, as it will benefit all constituents.

Referencing my in-class work, my story arc outlines a proper introduction into proving this data, and then ending with a concrete call-to-action. The common baseline for all readers to agree on is the downsides of heavy traffic, which will then go into the benefits of public transportation. Then, ending with qualifying information about current barriers that public transportation faces (such as government support and funding) will further fuel a call-to-action.

# The data

I have found two datasets to aid in the construction of my primary data visualization; the annual transit ridership per capita by U.S. cities (Think Tennessee), and vehicular commuting times by cities (GeoTab). I plan to use their interactive documents to aggregate a spreadsheet of my own, allowing myself to make a scatter plot comparing the two data points.

In addition, I have also located a traffic volumes dataset by state from the Office of Highway Policy. I hope to use this data to compare traffic counts for major American cities to their number of readily available transit options. However, I need to further investigate data that quantitatively assesses access to transit options in cities; I am thinking that I might need to locate statistics on each transit type separately.
