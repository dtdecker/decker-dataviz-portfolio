# Changes made since Part II

After submitting Part II, I received additional feedback from the course teaching team on the flow and clarity of my story in relation to my audience. Primarily, they suggested a clearer curation of my story towards either everyday users of public transit or policymakers, or to make distinct recommendations for both. Reflecting on the range of historical context and comparisons between cities that I provide in my story, I decided to direct it towards only U.S. residents, rather than writing to policymakers too. To emphasize this, I added more detail to my Call-To-Action, centered on three pieces of advice that the average citizen could follow to show support of public transit. I believe this was an effective choice for my story, because my original goal from the beginning was to justify the benefits of public transit towards both users and non-users.

In addition, I also worked to increase the clarity of my data visualizations in relation to how they supplemented my story, based on Part II feedback. To accomplish this, I provided additional paragraphs within my story's narrative explaining the origins of each dataset and contents of the visualization. In addition, I also mentioned and hyperlinked my sources within each paragraph, so that readers would easily be able to access the original sources without having to cipher through my bibliography. In doing so, I was able to refine and strengthen how each visualization supplements their relative parts of the story, while also providing additional context to readers to aid in their understanding.

## The audience

Even though my additional goal was to illustrate the benefits of public transit towards different kinds of urban residents (public transit users and personal vehicle commuters), I initially thought of my audience as both these residents and as policymakers with deicison-making power on transit funding and resources. After receiving crucial feedback on my Part II submission, I realized that this scope was too broad and weakening the final Call-To-Action of my story. At this point, I recognized that policymakers are likely to already be informed about the historical developments of public transit and its current funding shortcomings, since they have been primary drivers of either increased transit development or of funding cuts in the 21st Century. Therefore, I recentered my audience towards just urban residents, with the goal of increasing awareness about the history, benefits, and current threats to public transit. In doing so, I hope that readers will be motivated to voice support for public transit by contacting their local representatives and attending public interest meetings, while also incorporating diversified transit use into their daily lives.

## Final design decisions

My final design decisions were intended to reflect a cohesive theme and visual language between the data visualizations and supporting graphics of the Shorthand site. To begin, I selected an effective template which allowed for a variety of image displays and transitions. The dark blue and cream colors in the background ended up matching well with the Creative Commons images I was able to find via Unsplash and Pexels, which crafted an overall aesthetic. Then in the final revisions of my data visualizations, I chose to match these colors to complete the visual language. Overall, my visualizations use dark blue as the "default" or primary color, allowing the highlighting of certain data points while cream/light yellow is then used as a secondary color. I believe the use of these colors contribute to a strong visual cohesion throughout my story, while also aiding in the readability of my graphics.

# Final thoughts

Overall, I very much enjoyed the process of putting this project together, and I am proud of my final result. I was most excited for the capabilities of Shorthand to produce compelling visual narratives through graphical displays, and thought that was an effective way to tell my story while integrating data visualizations. Within the timespan of the project, the most challenging aspect was trying to research comprehensive datasets which effectively related to my story. If I had more time to develop the project, I would want to further research datasets related to public transit that are more relatable to a public audience. In doing so, I would also want to explore data visualizations which offered concise takeaways and an iconographic visual language, rather than focusing on indexes or total funding amounts. I think this would increase the effectiveness of my story relative to the desired public audience of urban residents.
